Frequently Asked Questions

Insights & Advisory Clarifications

Direct answers to critical questions on capital raising, IPO readiness, debt financing, M&A advisory, and strategic financial leadership.

🔍

A capital advisory firm helps businesses identify the right source and structure of capital for their growth plans. Companies typically need one when raising funds, restructuring capital or entering a new growth phase. The process covers assessing capital needs, choosing the instrument, preparing investor materials and managing the transaction to closure. Moneyfest supports equity, debt, private capital and strategic fundraising, from structuring to execution.

Companies can raise capital through equity, private equity or venture capital, debt, structured finance, strategic investors or public markets, depending on stage and requirements. The need usually arises with expansion, acquisitions or working capital requirements. The process starts with defining the requirement and use of funds, then selecting the route, preparing documentation and engaging investors or lenders. Moneyfest recommends the right route, structures the transaction and manages investor engagement.

Equity fundraising means raising capital by issuing shares in the company, without a repayment obligation. Companies use it to fund growth, strengthen the balance sheet or bring in strategic partners. The process involves agreeing valuation and dilution, preparing investor materials, approaching investors, negotiating terms and closing after due diligence. Moneyfest assists with strategy, valuation, structuring and investor coordination.

PE and VC funding involve investors providing capital in exchange for an ownership stake. VC typically backs early-stage, high-growth businesses, while PE invests in more established companies. Businesses consider it when they need growth capital along with investor expertise. The process runs from investor outreach and term sheet negotiation to due diligence and closing. Moneyfest prepares companies, positions them with relevant investors and supports execution.

Investors assess the business model, market opportunity, growth potential, financials, management, governance and scalability, along with valuation, exit potential and overall risk-return. Evaluation typically moves from initial screening to detailed due diligence, so companies should understand these criteria before approaching investors. Moneyfest helps identify gaps, build a clear investment case and present it in a form investors can evaluate efficiently.

Structured fundraising is a customised financing solution combining instruments such as equity, debt or convertibles. It suits companies where plain debt or equity does not fit their cash flows, dilution tolerance or timing. The process involves analysing the funding need, designing the structure, identifying investors and negotiating terms. Moneyfest advises on the structure and supports investor engagement and execution.

IPO readiness means meeting the financial, legal, governance and regulatory requirements of a public listing. Companies planning to list within the next few years should assess it early. The process starts with a gap assessment, then strengthens reporting, governance, compliance and corporate structure against a clear roadmap. Moneyfest helps assess readiness, close gaps and build the path to listing.

IPO preparation covers reviewing financials, structure, governance, compliance and disclosures, checking eligibility and building the equity story. It begins once a company decides to list and needs to withstand regulatory and investor scrutiny. It involves coordination across promoters, auditors, legal counsel and merchant bankers, leading to documentation and filings. Moneyfest supports preparation, positioning and coordination with intermediaries.

An SME IPO lists on the exchange's dedicated SME platform, while a Mainboard IPO lists on its main platform. They differ in eligibility, issue size, investor participation and compliance. SME listings also involve a market maker, and eligible companies can later migrate to the Mainboard. The right route depends on the company's scale, financials and growth plans. Moneyfest evaluates the profile and advises on the suitable route and preparation path.

A company may consider an IPO when it has established operations, a scalable model, sound financial and governance systems and a clear need for public capital or visibility. Listing is a long-term decision, so readiness and timing should be assessed well in advance, followed by preparation and execution. Moneyfest helps determine whether, when and through which route to list.

Debt funding is capital raised from lenders and repaid over an agreed period, generally with interest. Companies use it for working capital, capex, expansion and refinancing, through banks, NBFCs, private lenders, bonds or NCDs. The process involves assessing the requirement and repayment capacity, preparing lender documentation, approaching lenders and negotiating terms. Moneyfest advises on the right structure and supports lender engagement through to closure.

Debt syndication arranges a larger financing requirement through multiple lenders instead of one. It is needed when the funding size exceeds a single lender's appetite or risk needs to be spread. The process involves structuring the requirement, preparing a lender information package, approaching and negotiating with lenders and coordinating closure. Moneyfest structures the requirement, coordinates lenders and facilitates the transaction.

M&A advisory supports acquisitions, mergers, strategic investments and divestments. Companies need it when pursuing inorganic growth, entering new markets, restructuring or planning an exit. The process involves defining objectives, identifying counterparties, valuation, negotiation, due diligence and closing. Moneyfest assists at each stage, from evaluating the opportunity to coordinating the process.

Valuation considers financial performance, projected cash flows, comparable companies, industry multiples, assets and growth prospects. It is needed when raising capital, selling a stake, acquiring a business or planning a listing. The method, such as DCF, comparables or precedent transactions, depends on the business and transaction, and final value is shaped by commercial terms and market conditions. Moneyfest prepares the valuation analysis and uses it to support negotiation with investors or counterparties.

A Virtual CFO provides senior-level financial leadership, including planning, reporting, cash-flow management, MIS and controls, without the cost of a full-time CFO. It suits growing businesses needing stronger oversight, or preparing for fundraising or a listing. The engagement typically starts with a review of finance processes, then sets up reporting, controls and planning frameworks with ongoing support. Moneyfest provides this and aligns it with the company's growth and capital-raising plans.
